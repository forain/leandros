## Verdict: FIXED. The cause was cosmic-comp's per-process VFS fd table, full at 256/256.
Symptom: on aarch64/HVF virgl the 20th cosmic-term opened a blank window that ignored Super+Q.

The chain, measured on the base build with the new dump:
- The 20th terminal's main thread is in `ppoll(fd 256 POLLIN, no deadline)`.
  - Stack, symbolised against the gpu-stage libEGL and the sysroot libwayland: `eglSwapBuffers → dri2_swap_buffers → dri2_wl_swap_buffers_with_damage → wl_display_dispatch_queue → wl_display_poll`.
  - Mesa is waiting for a frame callback or buffer release.
- Nothing is pending on that socket. `[CONN] 205 … ab=0/4748 ba=0/27124` means the compositor read everything the client sent and sent nothing back unread.
  - So no wakeup was lost, and fd 256 is not a wrap. Every process's first socket is fd 256, and the healthy 19th terminal's socket is fd 256 too.
- The Ctrl-T `[VFS] heavy:` line shows cosmic-comp at `255/ev99` with 19 terminals and `256/ev100` with 20.
  - MAX_FDS was 256, the ceiling of the old [0, SOCK_FD_BASE=0x100) layout.
  - On virgl the compositor spends about 8 fds per client (about 100 at idle), so the 20th client's imports failed with EMFILE and nothing was logged. The compositor never presented the surface.
  - That produces the blank window, and the xdg close from Super+Q is never dispatched because Mesa owns the main thread.
- x86_64 Venus fit 20 terminals because its compositor fd cost per client is lower.
- The host was ruled out: the QEMU process stayed at about 145 open fds, and there was no host error.
- Unix connection pool: 209/256 were in use with 20 terminals (5 per terminal), so it was the next wall.

## Changes
| item | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| vfs `MAX_FDS` (per process) | 256 | 512 (FD_TABLES 5 → 10 MiB .bss) |
| socket fd range | [0x100, 0x2FE) | [0x200, 0x3FE), asserted < 0x400; still < 1024, so select() works |
| net `MAX_CONNS` | 256 | 512 (.data +2 MiB; `UnixConn` has its rings inline) |
| per-process EMFILE | silent | `[VFS] pid N: fd table FULL at 512 (EMFILE)` once per pid-in-a-row |
| conn pool empty | silent ENOMEM | `[NET] unix connection pool FULL` once |
| Ctrl-T dump | — | ppoll-without-deadline tasks print `pollfds` and `ret: addr@vma+file_off` |

Every socket-range check in the kernel (fcntl, close, poll, ioctl, sendfile, epoll probe) uses `SOCK_FD_BASE`/`EPOLL_FD_BASE` symbolically; the grep found no literal 0x100 or 256 fd bounds.
- dup/dup2/F_DUPFD are bounded by vfs `MAX_FDS`. dup2 to 512 gets EBADF, as before for 256.
- close_range walks 0..1023 VFS closes, unchanged.
- select caps nfds at 1024.

New `vfstest fd_layout_512` checks:
- dup2 of a pipe end onto 255, 256 and 511 carries data, and 512 gets EBADF.
- A socketpair lands in [0x200, 0x400), and both ppoll and pselect6 see it readable.
- A process holds at least 500 VFS fds before EMFILE, and the FULL line prints.

## Headroom (answers the orchestrator's question)
cosmic-comp idles at about 101 fds and costs about 8 per open virgl client. The old table gave 155 fds of headroom and the new one gives 411.
- Unleaked, 512 allows about 50 concurrent terminals.
- The virgl retention of about 0.75–0.9 fd per closed window eats the same budget. With N windows open, 512 lasts about (411 − 8N)/0.8 open/close cycles:
  - about 500 cycles with 1 terminal
  - about 430 with 8
  - about 270 closes before a 24-terminal desktop fails
  - before this change it was about 185 cycles with 1 terminal and about 115 with 8.
- Fixing compfdleak's retention is still needed for long sessions.
