# lane pihw2 (L8.8 Pi 5 hardware validation, continuing lane pihw) — 2026-10-26

Status: PARTIAL. One real bug FIXED (QEMU-verified); hardware validation of L8.8 BLOCKED by host-side capture.

## FIXED: init stray sweep SIGKILLed sshd (what looked like a "greeter restart loop")
- Exit 78 (no GPU) already prints the banner once and never respawns the greeter
  (userland/init/src/main.rs). The "... after 10 s, restarting in 1 s" line seen on the Pi is
  sshd's restart message, not the greeter's.
- Root cause: after greetd exits, `sweep_strays` kills every /proc entry whose ppid is init.
  A non-leader thread reports its process's parent, so sshd's tokio worker threads
  ("init: killing stray pid 24 (tokio-rt-worker)") matched; SIGKILL to one killed sshd
  ("sshd exited (status 9)"). Old QEMU logs under /tmp/ffsession: 1500+ such lines across runs
  (e.g. tests-pimem2d/serial-full.log lines 164-175), i.e. every no-GPU boot.
- Fix: exemptions apply to the thread-group owner (`/proc/<pid>/status` Tgid); a thread whose
  leader is alive is left to the leader's verdict.
- Guard: runtests.py fails a run whose serial-full.log contains "sshd exited (status 9)"
  (pattern present in the unfixed logs above).
- aarch64 regress (no GPU) with fix: PASS, banner printed once, no stray killed, no sshd exit.

## Serial "garbling": host side, not the Pi UART driver
- Two `cat` processes read /dev/cu.usbserial-1410 at once and both write /tmp/pihw-serial.log:
  an orphan from lane pihw (`timeout 3600 cat`, PPID 1, started 14:01) and the orchestrator's
  capture (`timeout 10800 cat`, 14:07). Each read() goes to one of them, and one writes at its own
  file offset, overwriting the other's bytes. Dropped runs are exactly 32 bytes
  ("PU RENDERER: the graphical login" missing from the banner), the reader chunk size.
- uart.rs review: TX waits for TXFF with a 10 ms deadline; bytes are dropped only after a
  10 ms stall (TX_WEDGED latch, counted in UART_TX_DROPPED). Nothing found that loses 32 bytes.
- Separate observation: Pi RX loses input characters when the Mac writes a line at full speed
  (shell lands in `>` continuation); sending 1 char per 20 ms works. Not investigated further
  (RX IRQ / rx-drain flow control on the Pi is the place to look).
