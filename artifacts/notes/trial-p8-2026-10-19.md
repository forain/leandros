- **Both launches pass.** RDD appeared within 3–6 polls (under 3 s), snd-sink and pipewire-pulse advanced, and exec_vcs rose.
- **The three broadcast parkers are gone.** sshd, netctl-events and login went from 20.4 % to 1.5–2.6 % in total. ALL-mask wakes dropped from
  9.6k/s to 34–181/s. In yt2, 148/s of those go to cosmic-comp in `sc -1`, against 192/s in the baseline.
- **The total CPU is not comparable with the baseline: YouTube served a 60 fps stream this time.** exec_vcs runs at 60/s instead of 30/s, the
  per-exec vcs time is the same (2.39 against 2.40 ms), and RDD's PRIME close doubled (1555/s against 776/s). So twice as many frames were
  decoded and passed on. Per frame, RDD cost the same or less (yt2 15.7 % / 60 against 8.2 % / 30). The rise in firefox and Web Content fits
  the doubled frame traffic. The ~20 % saved in the system services was taken up by that. A fair CPU comparison needs the stream pinned
  (MOZ_LOG, or a fixed quality/fps).
- yt1 only: the RDD process called `poll` 19.3k/s at 3.9 µs per call (immediate returns), against 2.8k/s in yt2 and 1.2k/s in hw2. That is
  where RDD's 24 % comes from (MediaPDecoder #1 at 11.9 %). It did not repeat in yt2. It looks like a spin on a ready fd or a zero
  timeout in the decoder, not a missed wake. Worth looking at if it comes back.
- New leftover stray wakes, on narrow tags but still frequent: console `login` getties (tid 21/23) at 426–455/s, and in yt1 pop-launcher
  `read` and WebExtensions `read` at 417–455/s with the same mask bit as the getty. Either the console-input tag shares a bloom bit with a
  busy pipe tag, or something wakes the console tag at ~450/s during playback. Its cost is small (login 1.4–2.1 %).
