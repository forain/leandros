# lane/fsio — 2026-10-09 — the boot7 USB-stick regression: root cause and fix

Branch `lane/fsio`, rebased on main `a8573d8` (r8169 + hda merged). Not
pushed. Input: `nvrm-boot6.log` (before) and `nvrm-boot7.log` (after
lane/nvstall) from the real PC (Kaby Lake 4C/4T, root f2fs on a USB 2.0 stick
13fe:4300, xHCI + BOT/SCSI, polled).

## 1. Root cause

**One global filesystem lock held across slow synchronous small writes, with
waiters spinning; the writes themselves were far more numerous than needed.**

### What the stick costs

- Lock waits in boot7 are quantised: 570-620, 1100-1200, 1670-1830, ~2250,
  ~3390 ms — multiples of **~575 ms**. That is a fixed-latency device
  operation, not console output (which varies) and not reads (the 63 MB GSP
  firmware read at ~12 MB/s, i.e. reads are fine). Best explanation: **a small
  (4 KiB, in-place) write on this cheap stick costs ~0.5 s** (FTL
  read-modify-write of an erase block). This is inferred; the new `[usbms]`
  line (below) measures it directly on the next boot.
- The real PC also has a **real 16550 UART at 115200 baud**: the [nvuapi]
  lines in boot6/boot7 are spaced at exactly **88.9 us/byte** (8N1 = 86.8),
  i.e. every kernel log line costs ~5-15 ms of CPU with IRQs masked.

### Where the small writes came from (all under `F2FS_MOUNTS`)

1. **klog** flushed every 2 s: tail page + header rewritten in place = at
   least two small writes per flush, ~1.15 s of every 2 s on that stick.
   Everyone's disk request queued behind it.
2. **f2fs checkpoints**: a whole-cache write-back (one 4 KiB write per dirty
   slot, in slot order) every 16 mutating requests AND synchronously after
   **every** create/mkdir/unlink/rmdir/symlink/link/rename (COSMIC's atomic
   config writes = create + rename = two checkpoints each).
3. **relatime** dirtied the inode block **and** its NAT block on the first
   read of every file (an image built on the host has atime == mtime), so the
   next checkpoint flushed dozens to hundreds of scattered in-place writes.

### Why everything got slow, even VT2 echo

- lane nvstall's `IoMutex` (ticket spinlock): every waiter spun with
  preemption disabled. boot7: 40458 contended waits, avg 16 ms, max hold
  **18.9 s**; with three CPUs spinning and the holder's CPU spinning on the
  USB poll loop, nothing else (the VT2 shell included) got a CPU.
- `[LOCKWAIT]` was printed while the reporter already **owned** the lock, and
  printing drained the console outbox at UART speed: each report extended the
  hold it reported (97 reports + suppressed ones).
- The console drain was unbounded: the CPU that drained kept writing other
  CPUs' lines as long as they were produced. **NMI sample** (boot7 WDOG,
  symbolised against the d0061ae x86_64 nvidia kernel):
  `putc_within <- write_tagged <- console_drain_outbox <- stage_bytes <-
  arch_serial_putc <- sys_mmap <- syscall_dispatch <- syscall_entry` —
  cpu0 deaf for 2 s writing the UART from an mmap's `[MMAP-SLOW]` print.
- boot6 vs boot7: same disease (boot6 had a 19.9 s mmap at exactly the same
  session-start point); boot7 added the under-the-lock reporting and turned
  "deaf" spinners into preempt-disabled spinners that still burn the CPUs.
- Lazy read-only shared mmaps (nvstall) are **not** the problem: fault-around
  (16 pages) + the shared page cache read far less than the eager copies;
  they stay.
- USB MSC reads were fine: the xHCI poll loop adds no per-command delay
  (irq_window every 256 polls is ~free); per-command cost is the device.

Reproduced in QEMU with the latency knob (`usbms.delay_us=1000
usbms.wdelay_us=500000`) on d0061ae: `[LOCKWAIT] ... waited 17927 ms ...
max hold ms 18008` — the boot7 18.9 s hold.

## 2. Fix

- **`sched::iolock::IoMutex` is a sleeping FIFO mutex** (ksync `KRawMutex`):
  waiters park, CPUs stay available. Not lockwatch-tracked (a sleeping holder
  migrates; a tracked holder would forbid nested sleeps). A caller that may
  not sleep falls back to a spin with IRQ windows and `[KSYNC] sleep in atomic
  context` names the site once (the debug assertion). `[LOCKWAIT]` and the new
  `[LOCKHOLD]` (hold >= 500 ms, naming the holder's executable) are printed
  after release.
- Callers audited (every f2fs entry): only three held something:
  execve's `EXEC_ARGV/EXEC_ENVP` spinlocks across prefaults (now `KMutex`),
  `close_by_port` from munmap under the address-space lock or from the
  scheduler-loop reap (now queued to `PENDING_CLOSE` when the caller cannot
  sleep and the table is busy; applied by the next locker).
- **f2fs bulk reads without the lock** (`read_unlocked`): under the lock the
  read is planned (inode, cached blocks copied, holes zeroed, uncached
  contiguous runs recorded); runs >= 32 KiB are read with the lock dropped and
  validated against a per-inode data generation bumped at every data/block-map
  change (write, truncate, free); a mismatch redoes the read under the lock.
  Covers read(2), exec/mmap faults (fault-around reads) and firmware.
- **Write-back**: `flush_all` writes dirty blocks in block order, coalescing
  adjacent blocks into one multi-block request (`blkdev::write_blocks`).
  Checkpoints from `maybe_flush` and from namespace operations are spaced
  >= 5 s; a periodic write-back (iostat task, 1 s) commits anything older.
  fsync/sync/remount-ro/unmount/shutdown still commit immediately; a
  checkpoint with nothing new is skipped. Crash consistency is unchanged in
  kind: until the checkpoint nothing of a deferred operation reaches the
  medium (evictions never pick a dirty slot while a clean one exists, and
  3/4-dirty forces a checkpoint), so a hard kill falls back to the previous
  checkpoint whole; it can lose the last ~6-10 s of changes (Linux loses 30 s).
- **lazytime atime**: the relatime update goes into the cached inode block
  without dirtying it (stat sees it; vfstest's relatime cases pass).
- **USB MSC**: per-disk sleeping lock (taken before the IoWindow); IoWindow
  (preempt-off spin) only when the caller cannot sleep; `usb_hcd::relax`
  spins 300 us then parks for 1/8 of the time waited (100 us .. 2 ms) — a
  0.5 s write no longer occupies a CPU. READ/WRITE commands up to 120 KiB
  (Linux's `max_sectors` 240 for USB 2.0; msc-tests updated, 26/26).
- **klog**: next flush due 10x the last flush's cost later (2..30 s).
- **console**: a drain writes at most what was queued when it started; the
  iostat task kicks the outbox every second.
- **xHCI interrupts** were not added: with sleeping waiters and park-polling
  they would save little; noted as future work.

## 3. Diagnostics to read on the next PC boot

- `[usbms] disk0 5000 ms: rd N cmds X KiB avg A ms max M ms | wr ... | busy B% |
  lockwait max L ms | lat <1ms:.. <4ms:.. ... >=1s:..` every 5 s when busy.
  **`wr avg` is the number that confirms or refutes the 0.5 s write
  hypothesis**; `busy` near 100% means the device is the bottleneck.
- `[fslock] F2FS_MOUNTS 5000 ms: N acquisitions, W waited (avg, max), held
  total T ms (max H ms); last klog flush F ms`.
- `[LOCKHOLD] F2FS_MOUNTS: pid P (/bin/x) held it H ms` / `[LOCKWAIT] ...
  slept S ms`: who held the filesystem and who waited.
- `[KSYNC] sleep in atomic context (...) at file:line` would name any
  remaining f2fs caller holding a spinlock (none seen in any run).

## 4. Measurements (Mac; aarch64 HVF, USB-stick root via qemu-xhci)

Emulated stick: `usbms.delay_us=1000 usbms.wdelay_us=500000` (1 ms per read
command, 0.5 s per write command). Baseline = d0061ae + only the latency knob
(spinning, 64 KiB commands). `fsbench` (new, userland) does the workloads; one
boot per row group, cold.

| workload | d0061ae | lane/fsio |
|---|---|---|
| read 143 MB file (libxul.so), cold | 3.39 s (41 MB/s) | 1.45-2.44 s (57-97 MB/s) |
| open+read 4 KiB of 817 icon files | **593.7 s** | 1.9-2.2 s |
| 200 appends to a root-fs file + sync | 129 s | 9-11 s |
| 8 processes mmapping 31 font files | 1.58 s | 0.59-0.70 s |
| 20 ms sleep probe during that storm: max lateness | 252 ms | 2-3 ms |
| longest F2FS_MOUNTS hold | 18.0 s | 5.0 s (a sync's checkpoint) |
| COSMIC (ffsession --nofirefox, virgl): greeter up | 544 s | **29 s** |
| COSMIC: desktop (cosmic-panel) up | not within 900 s | **77 s** |

Without the knob (QEMU speed) both are fast; lane/fsio's 143 MB read: 111 ms.
Console round trips through the driver stayed ~0.55 s (harness overhead) in
both; the sleep probe is the responsiveness measure.

Still slow on a 0.5 s-per-write stick: fsync-heavy apps. cosmic-term's start
was not seen within 600 s in the fsio COSMIC run: cosmic-panel issued a long
stream of config writes with fsync (98+ `[LOCKHOLD]`s, each a real
checkpoint). Our fsync is a whole-volume checkpoint (no per-file fsync); a
checkpoint still writes back with the lock held. A session process also
re-reads ~4.3 MB every 5 s (read(2) has no page cache). Both are follow-ups;
the next PC boot's `[usbms]` numbers decide how much they matter.

## 6. What the next PC boot should show

- The greeter and the session come up in tens of seconds instead of minutes;
  the VT2 shell echoes immediately (filesystem waiters sleep; a write in
  flight no longer occupies a CPU).
- Far fewer and shorter `[LOCKWAIT]`s; `[LOCKHOLD]` lines name whoever held
  the filesystem for >= 0.5 s (expect checkpoints from fsync-heavy COSMIC
  config writers, and the periodic write-back).
- `[usbms]` lines every 5 s while the stick is busy: read the `wr avg` (the
  ~0.5 s hypothesis) and `busy`. If writes are fast (a few ms), the remaining
  cost is elsewhere and the `[fslock]` hold numbers say whether it is still
  the filesystem.
- klog flushes every ~10-30 s instead of every 2 s on that stick (a crash
  may lose up to that much of the log tail; panics still flush).
- `[WDOG]` should not appear; if it does, its NMI sample is still printed.
